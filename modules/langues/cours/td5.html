Little, few, make, do

Two language sheets: “Little, a little, few, a few” (B1, Q004, with a key) and “Make or Do” (no key: suggested answers). The sheets only have exercises: the rules below are a reminder added for you.

Little or few? With or without a?

It depends on the noun: can you count it? You say two friends but not two waters: few friends, little water. Without “a” the meaning is negative (not enough); with “a” it is positive (some).

Uncountable noun (time, water, money)Countable plural noun (friends, ideas, days)
negative: not enoughlittle: We have little time left.few: I have very few friends.
positive: somea little: Would you like a little water?a few: We stayed for a few days.

Make or do?

Make: create, produce, buildDo: an activity, a task, a job
make a decision, a mistake, a complaint, an effortdo homework, the dishes, the cleaning, the accounts
make money, a deal, peace, arrangementsdo business, your best, your duty, a favour
make a phone call, an appointment, a date, a speechdo good, do harm, nothing to do with, do without
make sure, make fun of, make use of, make up your mindWhat do you do? How do you do? Will it do?
set expressions: make your bed, make a mess

Forms: make, makes, made, making; do, does, did, done, doing.

More examples

Added example: not from the course.

  • There is little milk in the fridge; we need to buy some. (uncountable, negative)
  • I've got a few questions about the exam. (countable, positive)
  • She made a lot of progress this term, and she did all the exercises.
