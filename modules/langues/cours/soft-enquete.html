Why employers want soft skills

Engineering schools mostly teach technical knowledge, yet companies ask for something else. The course shows it with a survey and three quotes.

The problem

“In most of the engineering institutions, more attention is on technical education related to a particular discipline.” Yet multi-national companies “are looking for more than the academic knowledge”: thinking skills, presentation skills, negotiation skills and time management skills.

The objectives of the lesson: raise students' awareness of the importance of soft skills; encourage students to improve their soft skills; clarify the difference between soft skills and hard skills.

Three quotes to remember

  • “75 percent of long-term job success depends on people skills, while only 25 percent on technical knowledge.” (Peggy Klaus)
  • “Of all the life skills available to us, communication is perhaps the most empowering.” (Bret Morrison, actor)
  • “Take advantage of every opportunity to practice your communication skills so that when important occasions arise, you will have the gift, the style, the sharpness, the clarity, and the emotions to affect other people.” (Jim Rohn, entrepreneur)

In the exam

Why should engineering students develop their soft skills? A short answer: Because companies look for more than academic knowledge. A survey in India shows that 70 % of students who had soft-skills training found better jobs, while hard skills contributed to only 30 % of success in getting employment.
