The skills of the future

A reading text with its exercises and the teacher's answer key: the typical format of a written exam (questions, true or false, multiple choice, vocabulary). The full text is on this page; the exercises are in the games; the answer key is in the next section.

The plan of the text

Paragraph 1: the world of work is changing. Paragraphs 2 to 6: one skill each (critical thinking, creativity, communication and collaboration, adaptability, emotional intelligence). Paragraph 7: conclusion. For each question, find the paragraph first, then the sentence that answers it.

The text

Paragraph 1. The world of work is changing at an unprecedented pace. Advances in artificial intelligence, automation, biotechnology, and digital communication are transforming the way people live and work. Some traditional jobs are disappearing, while new professions are emerging. As a result, success in the future will depend not only on what people know, but also on how effectively they can adapt to changing circumstances. Education and training will therefore need to focus increasingly on developing flexible and transferable skills.

Paragraph 2. One of the most important skills of the future will be critical thinking. In an age when enormous amounts of information are available at the click of a button, people must be able to distinguish reliable information from misleading or false claims. Critical thinkers question assumptions, examine evidence, identify contradictions, and reach conclusions based on logical reasoning. This skill will be particularly valuable because technology can provide information quickly, but it cannot always determine whether that information is accurate, relevant, or ethically appropriate.

Paragraph 3. Another essential skill will be creativity. Although machines can perform many repetitive tasks efficiently, human beings remain capable of imagining original ideas and approaching problems from unexpected perspectives. Creativity is not limited to artistic activities; it is also essential in science, business, engineering, education, and entrepreneurship. Employees who can generate innovative solutions and think beyond conventional approaches are likely to contribute significantly to organizations facing complex challenges.

Paragraph 4. Communication and collaboration will also become increasingly important. Modern workplaces often bring together people from different cultures, professions, and geographical locations. Employees must therefore be able to express their ideas clearly, listen actively, negotiate disagreements, and work effectively as members of a team. Digital communication has made collaboration easier in many ways, but it has also created new challenges. Misunderstandings can arise when people communicate through emails, messages, or video conferences rather than face to face.

Paragraph 5. Another crucial ability is adaptability. Technological developments can make certain skills obsolete within a relatively short period of time. Workers who depend entirely on one specific skill may struggle when industries change. By contrast, adaptable individuals are willing to learn new technologies, acquire new knowledge, and modify their methods when necessary. This does not mean that people must constantly abandon their existing expertise; rather, they need to develop a mindset of lifelong learning.

Paragraph 6. The future will also require strong emotional intelligence. Technical competence alone does not guarantee professional success. People need to understand their own emotions, manage stress, show empathy, and respond appropriately to the feelings of others. Emotional intelligence can strengthen relationships, improve teamwork, and help individuals resolve conflicts constructively. As workplaces become increasingly automated, distinctly human qualities such as empathy, compassion, and interpersonal awareness may become even more valuable.

Paragraph 7. Ultimately, the skills of the future are not simply a list of technical abilities. They represent a combination of intellectual, social, emotional, and technological capacities. Young people should therefore be encouraged to become curious, independent learners who are prepared to adapt to uncertainty. Schools, universities, employers, and governments all have a role to play in creating opportunities for continuous learning. The future of work may be unpredictable, but individuals who can think critically, communicate effectively, collaborate with others, innovate, and adapt will be better prepared to face its challenges.

How to answer

  • Answer the questions: start from the question (Because…), reuse words from the text, one or two sentences.
  • True or false: watch out for absolute words (no, only, exclusively, always); correct a false statement with the text.
  • Word formation: look at the word before the gap; after an adjective or an article you need a noun (-tion, -ity, -ment).
