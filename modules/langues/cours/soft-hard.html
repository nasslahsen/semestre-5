Hard skills and soft skills

Two engineers have the same degree and the same marks, but the company hires only one of them. What makes the difference is not on the transcript: that is what this lesson is about.

The idea

What you can do and prove with a test (programming, calibrating a device, reading a technical drawing) is a hard skill. How you work with other people and with yourself (listening, adapting, managing your time) is a soft skill. Hard skills are easy to measure; soft skills are not.

What the course says

Hard skills are:

  • teachable abilities;
  • skill sets that are easy to quantify;
  • learnt in the classroom, through books or other training materials, or on the job.

Soft skills: “The concept ‘soft skills’ is a sociological term referring to EQ, the Emotional intelligence quotient of a person.”

Soft skills are also known as: people skills, interpersonal skills, transferable skills, life skills, employable skills, personality development skills, behavioral competencies.

Useful language

  • I have strong hard skills in signal processing.
  • My main soft skills are teamwork and adaptability.
  • Hard skills are easy to quantify, while soft skills are linked to emotional intelligence.

Watch out

EQ (emotional intelligence) is not IQ (logical intelligence). Soft skills are about EQ.
