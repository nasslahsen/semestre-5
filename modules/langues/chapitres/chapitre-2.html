What a job interview is

What a job interview is

Before you prepare for an interview, you need to know what the employer is looking for.

Four definitions

A job interview is:

  • a formal conversation which occurs between a potential employer and a job applicant;
  • a selection process used by organizations to help determine whether a job candidate has the necessary knowledge, skills, and abilities to do the job;
  • a complex means of gathering relevant data about a candidate for a particular job position;
  • a mechanism for professional evaluation for employment.

Success depends on

  • hard skills: knowledge;
  • soft skills: good speaking skills, self confidence, a good use of non-verbal communication…;
  • thorough preparation.

A quick case

Added example: not from the course.

Salma knows MRI technology perfectly, but she speaks very quietly and never looks at the panel. Her hard skills are fine; her soft skills let her down: good speaking skills and non-verbal communication.

Pre-interview preparation

Pre-interview preparation techniques

“Thorough preparation”: the course divides it into six techniques to do before the day.

Six techniques

TechniqueWhat you do
1. Self analysisidentify your achievements and accomplishments, your career goals, your interests
2. Analyse your skillswhat makes you suitable for the position; what your strengths are
3. Research the organizationits activities, major areas of operation, recent developments, the factors making it successful
4. Job analysisthe duties and responsibilities of the job, the skills and abilities needed, chances of career advancement
5. Revise your subject knowledgehard skills (technical expertise), general awareness (national and international issues), recent developments in different areas
6. Develop the interview fileoriginal degrees and certificates, experience certificates, copies of your CV, any papers that might be needed

Remember the order

You first, then the company and the job, then your knowledge, then your papers.

A plan for a real post

Added example: not from the course.

For a biomedical technician post in a clinic: (1) note your maintenance internship; (2) list your strengths, rigour and electronics; (3) read the clinic's website and projects; (4) reread the duties of the post; (5) revise electrical safety standards; (6) prepare your original degree, internship certificate and three copies of your CV.

Types of interviews

Types of interviews

A phone call from HR, a panel of three people, six candidates around a table: these are not the same interview, and you don't behave the same way.

Five types

TypeWhat the course says
The screening interviewusually with someone in human resources; in person or on the telephone; checks if you meet the minimum qualifications for the job
The selection interviewthe employer knows you are qualified and needs to know if you have the personality to “fit in”; the step which makes people the most anxious
The group interviewseveral candidates at once; the interviewers try to separate the leaders from the followers and find out if you are a “team player”; the type of personality the employer wants determines the outcome
The panel interviewseveral individuals interview one candidate; stay calm, build rapport with each member, keep eye contact with each one while answering his or her questions
The stress interviewquestions so quickly that you don't have time to answer each one; weird questions, to see how you answer, not what; used to weed out the candidates who cannot handle adversity

Facing a weird question

Added example: not from the course.

“How many tennis balls fit in this room?”, asked very fast: stay calm and think aloud (Let me think: the room is about five metres long…). The interviewer watches how you react, not the exact number.

Typical questions and bad impressions

Typical questions and bad impressions

Some questions come up in almost every interview. And some attitudes are enough to make a bad impression, even with a good CV.

Typical questions

FamilyQuestions from the course
About youTell me about yourself. Why did you choose that particular degree programme? What would you consider your major achievements to date?
About the jobWhat interests you about this job? What do you know about this organisation? What experience have you had that is relevant to this post? How do you see your career developing (5 years)?
General knowledgenews and your field (only the title is on the slide)

What creates a bad impression?

  • poor personal appearance
  • negative attitude
  • lack of interest and enthusiasm
  • lack of preparation
  • poor knowledge of the expected role
  • failure to give concrete examples of skills
  • over emphasis on money/rewards

Weak answer, strong answer

Added example: not from the course.

What interests you about this job? Weak: The salary is good. (money first). Strong: I'm interested in medical imaging, and this post would let me maintain MRI scanners, which I studied during my internship. It shows interest, knowledge of the role and a concrete example.
