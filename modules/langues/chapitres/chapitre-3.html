The exceptional presenter

The exceptional presenter: OPEN UP

The course starts with a question: think of two exceptional presenters (a media personality, a politician, a professor, a business or religious leader). What do they have in common?

OPEN UP

The characteristics of an exceptional presenter are represented in the acronym OPEN UP: Organized, Passionate, Engaging, Natural, Understand your audience, Practice.

Why present?

The main objectives of a presentation: to inform, to entertain, to touch emotions, move to action. One presentation can aim at several of them.

Think about it

“Have you ever fallen asleep or felt bored during someone's speech, presentation or dialogue? If yes, give some reasons.” Typical reasons: the speaker reads, never looks up, speaks in the same tone, talks too long. Each one breaks a letter of OPEN UP.

An example

Added example: not from the course.

A blood donation campaign at the university: inform (how many bags are missing), touch emotions (a patient's story), move to action (“sign up today”).

Introduction and voice

Introduction and voice

The first seconds decide whether the audience will listen. The course gives seven tips for the introduction and three for the voice.

An attractive introduction

  • Be in time
  • Smile and look at the audience
  • Start slowly
  • Use a genuine opening
  • Look organized
  • State the main objective(s) of your presentation
  • Dress nicely

Your voice

  1. Be heard
  2. Air intake
  3. Vary your voice (speed, pitch and volume)

genuinesincere, real

An introduction that follows the course

Added example: not from the course.

Good morning everyone. Last year, our hospital lost two days of scans because of one broken cable. Today, my objective is to show you a simple maintenance plan that prevents this. I'll cover three points. A genuine opening, a clear objective, a visible plan.

Body language, visual aids, final tips

Body language, visual aids, final tips

The audience watches as much as it listens: your body and your slides speak too.

Body language

  1. Smile
  2. Maintain eye contact
  3. Smooth and natural gestures
  4. Keep your hands out of your pockets
  5. Keep your shoulders straight

Visual aids

  • Keep them simple
  • Minimize
  • Use large fonts
  • Use colors
  • Avoid complete dependence on technology

Final tips

  • Prepare well and know your topic
  • Use humor
  • Don't lecture
  • Get enthusiastic
  • Keep time for questions
  • Keep it short. Keep it focused. Keep it relevant.

The course presentation ends with: “Thank you for your attention… any questions?”

Before and after

Added example: not from the course.

Before: twelve lines of small black text on one slide. After: a title, three key words in large letters and one graph with a single colour for the key result. And if the projector breaks, you can still present the three key words from memory.

Watch out

Relevant means useful and connected to the topic. Watch out: it is a false friend for French speakers.
