Hard skills and soft skills

Hard skills and soft skills

Two engineers have the same degree and the same marks, but the company hires only one of them. What makes the difference is not on the transcript: that is what this lesson is about.

The idea

What you can do and prove with a test (programming, calibrating a device, reading a technical drawing) is a hard skill. How you work with other people and with yourself (listening, adapting, managing your time) is a soft skill. Hard skills are easy to measure; soft skills are not.

What the course says

Hard skills are:

  • teachable abilities;
  • skill sets that are easy to quantify;
  • learnt in the classroom, through books or other training materials, or on the job.

Soft skills: “The concept ‘soft skills’ is a sociological term referring to EQ, the Emotional intelligence quotient of a person.”

Soft skills are also known as: people skills, interpersonal skills, transferable skills, life skills, employable skills, personality development skills, behavioral competencies.

Useful language

  • I have strong hard skills in signal processing.
  • My main soft skills are teamwork and adaptability.
  • Hard skills are easy to quantify, while soft skills are linked to emotional intelligence.

Watch out

EQ (emotional intelligence) is not IQ (logical intelligence). Soft skills are about EQ.

What soft skills cover

What soft skills cover

“What are your soft skills?” In an interview or in the exam, you need to name some and recognise them in a situation.

A long list

Peggy Klaus, quoted in the course: “They cover a wide spectrum of abilities and traits: being self-aware, trustworthiness, conscientiousness, adaptability, critical thinking, attitude, initiative, empathy, confidence, integrity, self-control, organizational awareness, likability, influence, risk taking, problem solving, leadership, time management, and then some.”

The seven skills hiring managers look for in engineers

  1. Communication skills
  2. Interpersonal skills
  3. Problem solving skills
  4. Teamwork abilities
  5. Leadership and management skills
  6. Analytical skills
  7. Flexibility

Show a skill with one sentence

Added example: not from the course.

  • During my internship, the scanner software crashed before a test; I found the cause and restarted it safely. → problem solving
  • In our final-year project, I organised the tasks of our team of five. → leadership and management
  • When the project changed after two weeks, I adapted my part quickly. → flexibility

Why employers want them

Why employers want soft skills

Engineering schools mostly teach technical knowledge, yet companies ask for something else. The course shows it with a survey and three quotes.

The problem

“In most of the engineering institutions, more attention is on technical education related to a particular discipline.” Yet multi-national companies “are looking for more than the academic knowledge”: thinking skills, presentation skills, negotiation skills and time management skills.

The objectives of the lesson: raise students' awareness of the importance of soft skills; encourage students to improve their soft skills; clarify the difference between soft skills and hard skills.

Three quotes to remember

  • “75 percent of long-term job success depends on people skills, while only 25 percent on technical knowledge.” (Peggy Klaus)
  • “Of all the life skills available to us, communication is perhaps the most empowering.” (Bret Morrison, actor)
  • “Take advantage of every opportunity to practice your communication skills so that when important occasions arise, you will have the gift, the style, the sharpness, the clarity, and the emotions to affect other people.” (Jim Rohn, entrepreneur)

In the exam

Why should engineering students develop their soft skills? A short answer: Because companies look for more than academic knowledge. A survey in India shows that 70 % of students who had soft-skills training found better jobs, while hard skills contributed to only 30 % of success in getting employment.
