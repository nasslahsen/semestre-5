Interview questions

The sheet “Interview Questions – Practice” trains what chapter 2 announces: preparing your answers to typical questions. The sheet has no answer key, so the answers on this site are suggested answers. Exercises b, e and f come from a coursebook with audio (“Listen again”), and the audio was not sent.

Three types of questions

Every interview question asks for one of three things: talk about yourself (a), prove that you researched the company (b), show your skills and experience (c). Knowing the type tells you what to put in your answer.

Grammar for exercise e

  • after to, can, had to, able to, keen to, hope to: the base form (to motivate, can make);
  • after a preposition (about, in, before): the -ing form (worry about meeting);
  • after could be: the past participle, a passive (could be applied).

Practice 1: three types of questions

a: about yourself, your ambitions, your personality, your hobbies and interests. b: prove you have researched the job and company. c: demonstrate you have the skills and experience required.

  1. Can you tell us something about yourself?
  2. What experience do you have that is relevant for this position?
  3. What did you learn during your time at university?
  4. What do you see as your strengths?
  5. What do you think about our website?
  6. What attracted you to the position?
  7. How would your colleagues describe you?
  8. What sorts of projects did you work on in your last position?
  9. What university did you attend and why did you choose it?
  10. How do you typically approach new projects?
  11. What do you know about our company?
  12. Where do you see yourself in five years' time?

3 and 4 can go either way: answer 3 with your skills (like Alejandro) and it is c; talk about your personality in 4 and it is a.

Exercise e: Alejandro's improved answers

Box: apply, communicate, develop, gain, get (x2), lead, make, meet, motivate, put, work. Use the correct form.

  1. Can you tell us something about yourself? I put a lot of effort into everything I do… I had to organise matches and the players. As captain it was essential that I was able to effectively… the team to the final was a big achievement for me.
  2. What did you learn during your time at university? There were many elements of the degree that could be to the business world… This helped me to my prioritising skills and ability to to tight deadlines.
  3. What kinds of things do you worry about? I worry about deadlines and everything done on time.
  4. Would you say you're an ambitious person? I'm very keen to a job with a company such as yours where I can full use of my degree… and would hope to internal promotion.

Why these forms: Leading is the subject of the sentence; applied follows could be (passive); meeting, getting follow the preposition about.
